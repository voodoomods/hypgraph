Trust, but verify

Verify a bundle.

Drop a .bundle.json from hypgraph export. Your browser recomputes every SHA-256 in the chain and checks the rows against the events both ways: every row was written by an event and matches it field by field, and every event's row is there. Nothing leaves your machine.


What this proves — and what it doesn't

  • Proves: every event's hash is the SHA-256 of the exact string shown and each links to the one before; every node, version, link, withdrawal, retraction and lease in the bundle was written by an event and matches it field by field; and no row an event wrote is missing. Nothing was edited, added or dropped after it was written. The facts the explorer shows beside it (which nodes and links, their titles, authors, times, whether withdrawn) are checked against those rows too.
  • Doesn't cover: the standing (review state, support, at-risk, the one-line summary). That is what the exporting engine derived; re-derive it from the verified rows with hypgraph verify --standing. Records written before 0.7.13 didn't hash a few row fields into their events (a link's reason, say); the result lists those as not covered.
  • Doesn't prove on its own: that the whole record wasn't rebuilt from scratch. For that, paste the head hash published at the time (in a paper, a preregistration, a repository tag) above.
  • Scoped bundles (one investigation out of a shared record) redact the events outside it. Those can't be checked, and the result says so.
