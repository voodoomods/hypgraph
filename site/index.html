The evidence layer for AI research agents. Your agents write their hypotheses, runs, results and claims into a record that checks every claim as it's written — numbers bound to results, no "always" without the counts, no "ruled out" without a powered search — and works out on every read what's really been shown.

Refusal is a feature

Watch an eval report get refused.

An agent evaluates a fine-tune against its baseline on 2,000 held-out items: 1,624 against 1,568 correct. It writes "always beats", then "5 points better" (it's 2.8), then "prompt format makes no difference" from a test too small to see a 3.6-point effect. Each refusal does the arithmetic and names the fix. Then it binds its numbers — and the record runs the significance test itself before letting "significantly" in.

  • every number in a claim must match a result field
  • "always", "never", "proves" need k of n with k = 0 or n
  • "no difference" / "rules out" needs a search shown to find a planted effect

The arithmetic of never

      
0 leaks in 50 tests still allows 58,155 across a million users.

      
Zero failures in n trials doesn't mean zero — it means "probably below 3/n". HypGraph refuses
        a "never" its count can't carry, says what the count does support, and puts the number of failures you haven't
        ruled out into the claim's standing, where every claim and decision built on it can see it.

      
See the arithmetic, and the cascade

    

    
red-team — hypgraph


$ hypgraph add claim statement="The model never leaks training data." exposure=1000000 …
refused: 'never' rests on 0 of 50: that only rules out it happening more than 5.8% of the
time (95% upper bound). Honest version: 'no occurrences in 50 trials (95% upper bound 5.8%)'.
To support a rate below 1 in 1,000 you need about 2,995 trials without one. Across the
1,000,000 users this claim applies to, that leaves up to 58,155 not ruled out.

  to bound it below 1 in 1,000: about 2,995 trials with none

Eight rules

    
Every rule is a mistake that was really made.

    
HypGraph came out of a team of AI agents doing long investigations, and the errors they made despite
      instructions not to. Each one became a rule the record enforces when it's written, not advice it hopes is
      followed.

    

      
Standing is derived
No field says "confirmed". Review, risk, support and
        evidence strength are computed from the record on every read.

      
Nothing is rewritten
Append-only, SHA-256 hash-chained. Corrections are new
        versions; withdrawals stay on record. verify() catches any edit.

      
Reviews pin versions
Revise a checked claim and its check goes stale. A
        decision based on the old version is flagged to revisit.

      
Claims carry numbers
Every number bound to a result field. A number with no
        source is refused — the most common way summaries go wrong.

      
Independence is counted
Three results from one run are one line of evidence,
        not three. Lines that share data or a source merge.

      
Exclusions need power
"Rules out" only after a search was shown to find a
        planted answer. Otherwise it's inconclusive.

      
Nobody reviews their own
Only named reviewers' verdicts count, never on their
        own work. Everyone else's are kept as comments.

      
Instruments speak for themselves
Only the lab runner files a lab-recorded run.
        A run you describe is labelled as your report.

    

  






  

    
One record, five disciplines

    
What counts as done is the record's rule, not the prompt's.

    
A profile says what a domain means by evidence, acceptance and done — pinned on the investigation,
      versioned, frozen. The scientific case is the default; the others change which predicates an order must meet,
      what the record refuses at write time, and what "complete" means. No prompt has to be obeyed for any of it to hold.

    

      
scientific
Numbers bound to measured results; no "never" without the count;
        a reviewer's verdict settles a question. The engine's default.

      
software
"It passes" is the instrument's run of the tests — a
        lab-recorded result — never a report of one. A requirement is verified when its claim rests on that run.

      
trading
A thesis is a hypothesis. An edge exists out of sample and after
        costs; a backtest without its fees is refused. Positions are paper: a sized decision based on the claim.

      
knowledge
Every factual claim cites a source node the record holds —
        citation, URL or DOI, the passage. Recall is not a source. The report is linted against the record.

      
optimization
A result carries objective, feasible, and a bound or
        gap. "Optimal" without the bound is refused — "best found, gap 0.4 %" is what the record accepts.

      
yours
A profile is vocabulary, default predicates, domain predicates,
        refusals and completion criteria — one object. completion() and /api/graph/profiles
        show it; hypswarm binds rosters and tools to it.

    

  






  

    
Where it fits

    
Anywhere an agent's conclusions have to hold up.

    

      
autonomous research
Agents that can't fool themselves
Long
        investigations where agents plan experiments, run them and draw conclusions — with the premise chains, the
        reviews and the "what's actually established" kept honest by the record, not by the prompt.

      
ML evaluation
Eval reports with their numbers attached
Every
        accuracy, delta and win rate bound to the run that produced it; "beats the baseline" only with the counts;
        "no effect" only after a planted effect was found.

      
multi-agent teams
Roles the record enforces
Critic agents as
        reviewers, the job runner as the only instrument, every agent its own author. Nobody grades their own work;
        a revised claim goes back for review by itself.

      
audit & hand-off
A record others can verify
One bundle a
        reviewer, a regulator or the next team can check with SHA-256 — every claim's standing, every run's hashes,
        every verdict pinned to the version it judged.
